# Everything it does

One window, and inside it as many Spectrums as you like, the hardware of forty years plugged into them by
hand, every game in the catalogue a search away, and a speed dial that goes to three hundred times real
time. This page is the whole of it, in pictures.

## The desk

![Three machines at three speeds](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fpetrola/oozx/main/doc/wiki/img/desk-tiled.png)

| | |
|---|---|
| **As many machines as you want** | Each window is a whole Spectrum with its own model, speed, sound, pokes and screen. Three at once, at 100 %, 309 % and 9,775 %, is an ordinary afternoon |
| **Windows you clip together** | Drag a window against a machine and it snaps on: it follows it, stays in front of it, closes with it |
| **The session comes back** | Quit and reopen: the same windows, and the machines *still running where they were* |
| **A look you can live with** | Dozens of themes. Choosing one tries it on and asks — if you say nothing it puts the old one back, so an unreadable theme cannot trap you |
| **Full screen, zoom, borders** | 1×, 2×, 3×, fullscreen with Escape to leave, and the border on or off |

![Dragging a device onto a machine plugs it in](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fpetrola/oozx/main/doc/wiki/img/clipping.gif)

**Where a window is, means something.** The settings window configures *what it is clipped to* — let go of
every machine and it configures what the next one will start with, and says so in orange.

![The settings, clipped onto a machine](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fpetrola/oozx/main/doc/wiki/img/settings-tabs.png)

## Hardware you plug in

Two dozen peripherals. Each one is a window with the front of the thing on it, and **clipping that window
onto a machine is what plugs it in**. Unclip it to unplug it; carry it to another machine and it is that
machine's now.

![Drawing with the mouse in the Art Studio and printing it](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fpetrola/oozx/main/doc/wiki/img/multi-devices.gif)

## The ones you will not find elsewhere

1. **Several Spectrums on one desk**, each its own machine, each at its own speed, all in one process.
2. **Plugging hardware in by dragging a window onto another.** No dialog, no checkbox: the window *is* the
   board, and where it is, is whether it is plugged in.
3. **Front panels, not settings pages** — motor lamps, eject buttons, write-protect tabs, a red freeze
   button, paper coming out of a printer.
4. **Take Over**: stop somebody's recording mid-frame and keep playing from exactly there.
5. **256 colours on a 1984 game**, over photographs, with the game's code untouched.
6. **A library that knows what your files are** by what is inside them, not by their names.
7. **Three hundred times real time with sound and picture on** — and the processor swappable while it runs.
8. **A settings window that configures whatever you clip it to**, or the next machine if you clip it to
   nothing.
9. **The whole ZXInfo catalogue and the RZX archive inside the emulator**, with no browser and no files.
10. **A new peripheral is a jar you drop on the classpath** — no core to edit, nothing to register by hand.
