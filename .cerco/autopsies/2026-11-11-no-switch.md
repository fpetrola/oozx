## Cause

<!-- Not what the rule says, but why the code was written that way: the concept, responsibility or

EmulatorCore.ownSettings said the machine's four settings (model, ROM set, processor, turbo) through Settings'
Described and Values, which are asked by the name of a property. Having no object for one property, it answered every
question with a switch over the four names: one for the description, one for the choices, one to read and one to
tell, plus a test for the type, with a cast in the telling. Each property was spread across five switches, and adding
one meant adding a case to each. Settings.Property now holds a property whole and Settings.of(name, properties...)
answers the name-keyed questions from them, so EmulatorCore lists its four properties once each.

model: concept-without-a-class
